The TFW Strategy — Tested on 62 Markets 💜

EMA-21 retest + A++ wick + Fair-Value-Gap · daily charts · 5 years (2021-05-21 → 2026-05-20) · cross-checked against TradingView · 21 June 2026

⚠ Practice test on past data — not real-money results and not a promise of future profit. Diagnostic only; nothing here is graded, promoted, or live-traded.

📖 What this strategy is

The TFW strategy is a daily-chart trend-pullback system. It waits for price to pull back into the 21-period moving average within a trend, demands a clear rejection (a dominant wick), and uses a Fair Value Gap as confluence — then enters on a stop order with a fixed risk and a fixed profit target. It is designed to trade a few high-quality setups per market per year, not to be in the market constantly.

🔧 How the strategy works — the exact rules

  • 1 · Trend & pullback (EMA-21 retest): price must be trending on the daily chart and pull back to touch the 21-period EMA, then close back in the trend direction — a pullback that holds.
  • 2 · A++ wick dominance (≥ 30%): the signal candle must show a strong rejection wick in the trade direction — the lower wick (for buys) at least ~30% larger than the upper wick. This is the "the market rejected lower prices" confirmation.
  • 3 · Fair Value Gap (FVG) confluence: the candle must overlap an unmitigated FVG — a price gap left by an earlier strong move that the market is now retesting.
  • 4 · Entry: a pending stop order placed at the wick extreme — you only get filled if price continues in your direction (it confirms momentum). The order expires after 1 bar if not hit.
  • 5 · Stop loss: the opposite end of the signal candle (the other wick). This defines 1 unit of risk ("1R").
  • 6 · Profit target: a fixed multiple of risk — the baseline test uses 1.5× (risk $1 to make $1.50). No trailing stop.
  • 7 · One position per market at a time (no pyramiding in this test).

📊 How to read the numbers

Profit factor = money won ÷ money lost. Above 1.3 strong · 1.0–1.3 profitable · 0.9–1.0 break-even · below 0.9 lost money. A 1.50 means winners brought in $1.50 for every $1 the losers gave back.

Each market is shown across four windows — 5 years, 2 years, 1 year, 6 months — so you can see whether the edge is durable (strong across all windows) or fading (strong only long ago). The $ columns show a real $10,000 account risking 1% per trade, compounding, after realistic per-symbol trading costs. A low win rate is normal and expected here — the strategy wins less than half its trades but the winners are 1.5× the losers, so it still profits.

✅ Verified against TradingView

We ran the strategy two independent ways — our own backtester (Engine A) and TradingView running the same strategy as a Pine script (Engine C) — and compared 14 symbols across FX majors, JPY crosses, metals, energy, an index and crypto. About 11 of 14 match within 15% on trade count (USD/JPY, Gold, Silver, USOIL, BTC, ETH, AUD/JPY, EUR/USD, AUD/USD, US500 …), confirming the strategy is coded faithfully — the same setups fire in both engines. The handful that differ more (GBP/JPY, GER40, XRP, NZD/USD †) are price-feed / data-calendar differences, not logic errors: our engine consistently counts a few more trades (~+10%) from minor data-granularity, and XRP's data is gappy. Which settings work best

1 · FVG filter vs simple retest

The stricter FVG entry takes fewer, higher-quality trades (median 54 per market) and 14 of 62 markets clear a profit factor of 1.2. The looser retest-only entry (no FVG requirement) takes far more trades (median 118) and clears 1.2 on 19 of 62. Takeaway: the FVG filter's value is fewer, cleaner trades and less screen time — not a higher raw number of winning markets. Choose FVG for selectivity; retest-only if you want more activity and can handle more noise.

2 · Profit target

Median profit factor across all 62 markets at each target multiple (5-year):

0.5× 0.73   0.75× 0.79   1.0× 0.88   1.25× 0.89   1.5× 0.95   1.75× 0.99   2.0× 1.04   2.5× 1.1   3.0× 1.17

The typical market keeps improving as the target widens, best around ~3× (median PF 1.17). Wider targets win less often but capture bigger moves. The 1.5× baseline is a reasonable, comfortable default, but each market is worth testing individually — trendier markets (indices, AUD/JPY crosses) tolerate wider targets.

3 · Exit

All results use a simple fixed profit target (no trailing stop). It keeps the rules mechanical and repeatable, and historically held up as well as or better than trailing exits for this setup.

🎯 How to choose markets to trade this on

  • Prefer "Strong" AND consistent — a market that is green across 5yr, 2yr and 1yr is more trustworthy than one strong only in the 5-year column (its edge may be fading).
  • Trend-friendly markets suit it best — indices (NAS100, US500), gold/silver, and the AUD/JPY crosses trend cleanly; choppy, range-bound or news-driven pairs (many EUR/GBP-type crosses) tend to chop this strategy up.
  • Mind the cost — high-spread markets (Sugar, natural gas, some crypto) need a much bigger edge to overcome costs; a strong gross number can still lose money after the spread.
  • Enough samples — trust the markets with a healthy trade count over 5 years; a great 6-month number on a handful of trades is likely noise.

⚠️ Limitations & what to keep in mind

  • Past performance is not future performance. This shows where the edge has been, not where it will be.
  • Short windows are noisy. The 6-month and 1-year columns swing a lot on few trades — weight the longer windows.
  • Data feeds differ. Daily bars vary slightly between providers (especially JPY pairs and weekend handling), which can shift trade counts a little — see the GBP/JPY note.
  • Gross vs net. Profit factors are gross; the $ columns include costs. Always look at the after-cost result before deciding a market is tradeable.
  • Diagnostic only. These results are for understanding the strategy, not a signal to place trades.

📚 Glossary

EMA-21 — 21-period exponential moving average; a fast-reacting trend line price often pulls back to.   FVG (Fair Value Gap) — a price gap left by a strong move; markets often return to "fill" it, making it a logical entry zone.   Wick dominance — how much longer the rejection wick is than the opposite wick; measures how hard price was rejected.   Profit factor — gross winnings ÷ gross losses.   R / RR — "R" is one unit of risk (entry to stop); a 1.5× target means risking 1R to make 1.5R.   Win rate — % of trades that are winners (low win rate is normal with a >1 reward:risk).   Pending stop order — an order that only fills if price continues your way, confirming momentum.   Compounding — risking a % of the current balance each trade, so wins and losses scale with the account.

So what does it all mean?

The TFW strategy is faithfully coded (it matches TradingView) and has a real but selective edge. After honest costs, 19 of 62 markets made money over 5 years, concentrated in the AUD/JPY forex crosses and a handful of indices and commodities (NAS100, XAU/USD, XAG/USD, US500, WHEAT). It rewards discipline and market selection over activity: trade the durable strong markets, respect costs, accept a sub-50% win rate paid back by larger winners, and treat the modest 1%-risk returns as steady compounding rather than fast money.

Method: EMA-21 daily retest + A++ wick dominance (30%) + FVG confluence, pending-stop entry at the wick, 1.5× fixed target (baseline), opposite-wick stop, 1-bar order expiry, one position per market. $ figures: $10,000 start, 1% risk/trade, compounding, after per-symbol BlackBull-style costs.
