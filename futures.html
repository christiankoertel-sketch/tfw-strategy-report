What "realistic futures" means here

The exact same TFW strategy on real exchange futures contracts (CME, COMEX, NYMEX, CBOT, ICE) in TradingView, sized the way a real futures account works: a $100,000 account risking 3% per trade, buying whole contracts at each contract's real point value (ES = $50/pt, GC = $100/oz, CL = $1,000/bbl, …), with real per-contract commissions (~$4 round-turn) and 1-tick slippage. Returns compound. This is the closest thing to what trading these on a funded futures account would have done.

Why risk-based sizing matters: futures contracts are large, so you size how many whole contracts ≈ 3% risk rather than a fixed number. Done this way the edge carries over from spot. (A naive "1 contract every trade" instead blows the account — sizing is everything.)

Honest caveats

  • † Grains & coffee ($ not shown). CBOT corn/wheat/soybeans and ICE coffee are cents-quoted; TradingView mis-reports their contract point value on the continuous series, so the dollar P&L came out off by ~100×. Their profit factor and trade counts are still valid and shown — the dollar columns are not.
  • Continuous contracts. These use TradingView's 1! front-month continuous series, which stitch contracts at rollover; un-adjusted rollover gaps can occasionally create a signal that wouldn't exist on a single contract.
  • Account size matters. Results are for a $100k account at 3% risk. A smaller account couldn't trade some full-size contracts at all; micros (MES, MGC…) would be needed.
  • Diagnostic only — for understanding the strategy on futures, not a signal to trade.

Method: EMA-21 daily retest + A++ wick (30%) + FVG confluence, pending-stop entry, 1.5× target, risk-based whole-contract sizing at 3% per trade on $100k, real point value + ~$4/contract commission + 1-tick slippage. Engine C = TradingView. Window 2021-05-21 → 2026-05-20.
